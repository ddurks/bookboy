Getting it onto a Game Boy

You need a flashcart — a cartridge with a microSD slot. Copy the .gba onto the card (FAT32, any folder), slot it in, and pick it from the cart’s menu. Your reading position saves automatically (the cart writes a .sav beside the ROM). In the reader: A/→ page forward, ← back, ↑/↓ jump chapters, SELECT cycles palettes, B returns to your library.

EZ-Flash Air$40 The current budget pick. One extra step: after copying, you “write” the ROM from SD to the cart’s internal storage (~2 min for a full 32 MB library) before playing.
EZ-Flash Omega DE$70–90 Discontinued in 2025 but the nicest EZ-Flash — instant loading, saves without a battery. Grab remaining stock if you see it.
EverDrive GBA Mini (krikzz)$99 The premium option: fastest, most reliable, drag-and-drop. Buy from krikzz.com or Stone Age Gamer.
SuperCard clones$15–25 Avoid — 2005-era design, needs ROM patching, 2 GB card limit, fragile saves. The EZ-Flash Air is worth the extra $15.

Buy from the makers’ listed resellers (Handheld Legend, SenkoGames, Retro Game Repair Shop, krikzz.com) — marketplaces are full of lookalike clones. Note the EZ-Flash Junior is GB/GBC-only and won’t run GBA ROMs.

What plays it: any Game Boy Advance — the backlit SP (AGS‑101) is the best screen of the era; the frontlit SP (AGS‑001) works great with the high-contrast palette. Also the Game Boy Micro, and the GBA slot of a DS/DS Lite. The Analogue Pocket can even run the .gba straight from its own SD card via the community openFPGA GBA core. No hardware? mGBA (desktop), Delta (iOS), or Lemuroid (Android) run it perfectly.
